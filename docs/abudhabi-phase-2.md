# Abu Dhabi — arcades, palace forecourt and skyline

This pass develops the first-phase Abu Dhabi scene without altering Dubai's district or global lighting.

## Visible changes

- Six open pointed-arch bays in two wings flank the mosque approach. Eight slender columns, pale capitals, restrained bronze collars, beveled arch edges and recessed ceilings create real depth and cast shadows. The central entrance remains open.
- The palace gains four raised planted parterres, a tiled forecourt and a six-unit central approach connected to the entrance. Low groundcover, small cream flowers, relocated shrubs and four vertical ornamental trees create distinct planting layers.
- Source shrubs that overlap the new forecourt move into the beds. Their original collision rectangles are removed only while refinement is enabled and restored with their original transforms when disabled.
- Tall ordinary buildings use five footprint-contained massing families: shoulders, frames, offset setbacks, narrow blades and sloping crowns. Lobby bases, recessed roof equipment and vertical fins replace the repeated flat rectangular roofline. Existing landmark models remain identifiable.
- A dedicated palace forecourt review camera supports repeatable visual and walking checks.

## Validation

- Syntax checks, 90 tests and production build pass; the existing Three.js bundle-size advisory remains.
- Tests check the clear central garden axis, reversible collision relocation, source asset ownership and scene cleanup.
- Browser review covers overview, mosque, Corniche, palace, forecourt and skyline. Keyboard walking crosses the palace axis and the Corniche; all six Abu Dhabi landmark arrivals/checkins pass, followed by material toggles and a Dubai waterfront return.

## Remaining detail

The original mosque dome/minaret geometry and palace façade still need finer modeling. Wider palace grounds, pavement transitions beyond the new forecourt, unique landmark tower surfaces and larger landscape composition remain future passes. This is a procedural miniature, not a measured architectural reconstruction. Mobile frame rate has not been benchmarked.
